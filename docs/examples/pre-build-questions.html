Example 1 · A behavior choice

We're making: shared notes for a small team.

Already decided: notes save automatically.

Scenario: Maya opens a note on the train and starts typing. Before the note finishes saving, her internet connection drops for two minutes.

What does Maya see on the page, and can she keep typing?

Proposed options

Maya is writing → the connection drops
↓
A · Keep writing

Keep a draft on this device. Show “Waiting to sync”.

Connection returns → try saving again.
B · Pause editing

Keep what she has typed. Show “Reconnect to keep writing”.

Connection returns → editing resumes.

Both keep the text already typed. A lets Maya continue while offline; B makes her wait. “Waiting to sync” is not a successful save.

My pick: A. Maya can finish her notes. The drawback: this device holds the only copy of new text until syncing succeeds.

Which should happen? Try A or B.

With A: Maya keeps writing. Her new text appears on other devices only after a successful sync.

With B: Maya keeps the text already typed, but cannot add more until the connection returns.

Example 2 · An access choice

We're making: shared notes for a small team.

Already decided: Maya owns a note and invites Leo to read it.

Scenario: Leo opens Maya's note, copies its link, and sends it to Sam. Sam has not been invited but clicks the link.

Does Sam see the note, or a message saying they need access?

Neither option lets Sam edit the note.

Proposed options · Reading this note

A checks invitations; B treats possession of the link as permission to read.

My pick: A. Forwarding a link cannot accidentally share private notes. The drawback: Maya must invite each new reader.

Who should be able to read it?

With A: Sam sees an access message. Maya must invite Sam before the note opens.

With B: Sam can read the note immediately, and can forward the link to another reader.

Example 3 · A screen choice

We're making: shared notes for a small team.

Already decided: the home screen lists your recent notes.

Scenario: Maya opens the app on her phone to find last week's meeting notes. Several newer notes appear above the one she wants.

Should Maya see more notes at once, or fewer notes with more room to read and tap each one?

Illustrative wireframes · Not running app screenshots

A · Compact list
  • Team meeting
  • Launch checklist
  • Customer feedback
  • Ideas for next week
  • Design notes

B · Roomier list
  • Team meeting
  • Launch checklist

The same notes and actions, with different spacing. A fits more rows; B gives each row more room. Final counts depend on screen size and text length.

My pick: B for a team mostly using phones. The drawback: more scrolling to reach older notes.

Which would suit your team better?

With A: prioritize fast scanning. Touch targets and keyboard access still need to meet the accessibility requirements.

With B: prioritize comfortable reading and tapping. Fewer notes fit before you scroll.
